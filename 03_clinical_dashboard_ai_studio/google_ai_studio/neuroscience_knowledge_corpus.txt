================================================================================
NEURORETRIEVAL AUTHORIZED KNOWLEDGE CORPUS: EEG BIOMARKERS & SCHIZOPHRENIA
================================================================================

[SECTION 1: EEG BIOMARKERS, COROLLARY DISCHARGE, AND SENSORY ATTENUATION]

1.1 Sensory Attenuation and the Corollary Discharge Circuit:
A fundamental feature of healthy neurocognitive functioning is the brain's ability to distinguish between sensory events that are self-generated (e.g., vocalization, self-initiated button presses) and those externally generated by the surrounding environment. When motor cortex plans an action, it issues an efference copy (corollary discharge) to sensory cortices (such as primary and secondary auditory cortex). This predictive signal dampens or attenuates sensory processing for expected self-generated stimuli.

1.2 The N100 Event-Related Potential (ERP):
The N100 (or N1) ERP wave is a negative auditory deflection peaking ~100 milliseconds post-stimulus onset, originating within Heschl's gyrus and planum temporale. In healthy controls, N100 amplitude is markedly attenuated when listening to self-generated tones compared to identical passive computer-delivered tones. In patients with schizophrenia, studies show a prominent failure of sensory attenuation: N100 amplitudes remain unattenuated during self-initiated acoustic events.

1.3 Pathophysiology of Auditory Verbal Hallucinations:
Impairment in corollary discharge circuitry explains positive auditory symptoms: when internal thoughts and subvocal speech fail to be tagged as self-generated via sensory attenuation, they are experienced as alien, externally originating voices. Similarly, failure to differentiate internal sensorimotor intentions from external agency produces delusions of control and thought insertion.

1.4 Additional Electrophysiological Markers:
- P300 (P3b) Deficits: Reduction in P300 amplitude reflecting impaired working memory updating and stimulus evaluation.
- Mismatch Negativity (MMN): Attenuated pre-attentive sensory memory responses indicating cortical NMDA-receptor hypofunction.
- Gamma-Band Oscillations (40 Hz ASSR): Impaired phase synchrony across local parvalbumin-positive GABAergic interneuron networks.

[SECTION 2: SCHIZOPHRENIA SPECTRUM CLINICAL PSYCHOEDUCATION]

2.1 Diagnostic Framing & Neurobiology:
Schizophrenia is a complex neurodevelopmental condition involving disruptions in neural synchrony, dopaminergic pathways (hyperactive mesolimbic D2 transmission linked to positive symptoms; hypoactive mesocortical D1 transmission linked to negative/cognitive symptoms), glutamatergic signaling, and frontotemporal-thalamic connectivity.

2.2 Symptom Profiles:
- Positive Symptoms: Hallucinations (predominantly auditory), delusions (persecutory, referential, grandiose, somatic), disorganized thought/speech, catatonic motor behaviors.
- Negative Symptoms: Avolition, anhedonia, asociality, blunted affect, alogia.
- Cognitive Deficits: Working memory limitations, executive dysfunction, reduced processing speed.

2.3 Recovery-Oriented Psychoeducation:
Early intervention in psychosis (EIP), combined psychosocial support, psychoeducation, and personalized medical care facilitate substantial functional recovery, personal autonomy, and quality of life.

[SECTION 3: EVIDENCE-BASED COPING STRATEGIES & GROUNDING PROTOCOLS]

3.1 Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Psychosis (CBTp):
Focuses on reducing emotional distress and disruption caused by symptoms rather than attempting to forcefully eradicate perceptions. Encourages gentle reality-testing, alternative normalizing explanations, and distress tolerance.

3.2 5-4-3-2-1 Somatosensory Grounding Exercise:
Used during acute sensory overload, anxiety, or intrusive thoughts to reconnect with physical reality:
- 5 things you can visually observe in the immediate room.
- 4 tangible textures/objects you can physically touch (feet on floor, desk texture).
- 3 real ambient auditory sounds in the room.
- 2 ambient scents.
- 1 physical taste (sip of cold water or mint).

3.3 Autonomic Respiration (Box Breathing & Physiological Sigh):
- Box Breathing: 4s inhale, 4s hold, 4s exhale, 4s pause.
- Physiological Sigh: Double inhale through nose, prolonged slow exhale through mouth to restore parasympathetic balance.

3.4 Auditory Sensory Coping Techniques:
- Subvocal disruption: Softly humming, singing, or reading aloud physically activates vocal musculature, competing with internal subvocal auditory verbal hallucinations.
- Acoustic distraction: Listening to calming instrumental music or podcasts with headphones.
